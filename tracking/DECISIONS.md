### 2026-08-26 — packet P11: what counts as "the debtor opened the link" in a text-only world
**The question the spec never had to answer.** Master doc §7.2 gives the Sentinel a link-open timer ("sent != delivered != opened") and `link_timed_out()` implements it correctly. It presumes a click event exists. In this simulation it does not — there is no pixel, no redirect, no webhook. So the runner had `track_link_sent` and no way to ever say "opened", which is why every instrument soft-refused itself at 48h (packet P10's finding, BUILD_LOG 2026-08-26).

**Decision: any inbound message from the debtor marks every instrument still inside its window as opened.** Not "a reply that agrees", not "a reply whose extraction is L1–L3", not "a `confirm_mandate` move" — *any* message. Three reasons, in order of weight:
1. **It is the closest true statement available.** A human who types a reply into a thread whose last message carried a payment link has received and read that message. That is precisely, and only, what "opened" claims. Anything narrower would be inferring intent from a delivery signal.
2. **Agreement is already decided one layer up, and better.** Whether the debtor said yes is answered by the real extraction and the frozen persona move, feeding `ledger.process_event`. If the Sentinel also got a vote on it, a delivery-layer heuristic could contradict a judgment-layer decision — the inversion CLAUDE.md law 1 exists to prevent, just with a rules engine instead of an LLM.
3. **It is the version that cannot rot.** Taking the signal in `_inbound` — the one funnel every debtor message passes through — means a persona branch added later cannot forget to report engagement. A per-branch rule ("mark it in the `confirm_mandate` case") is the same shape of bug as the one being fixed.

**What still times out, deliberately:** `decide_reply_move` → `silence` and `decide_mandate_move` → `ignore` send no message at all, so nothing marks their instrument and the 48h window closes as a soft refusal exactly as before. That is the correct reading of both moves — "ignore" in the mandate table means the debtor did not answer the offer, not that they answered it dismissively. Pinned by `tests/test_integration.py::test_true_silence_still_soft_refuses_after_48h` against the run's own instance (INV-003, day 21 offer → `ignore` → day 23 soft refusal).

**Two smaller calls inside the same fix:**
- **The runner keeps its own index of outstanding instruments (`_open_instruments`) rather than reading the Sentinel's `link_sent_at` / `link_opened` sets.** The Sentinel stays the single source of truth about what happened to a link; this dict only answers "which windows are still open for this entity", which is scheduling state the runner already owns (it is the same object that schedules the `link_timeout` beat). Nothing in `engine/action/sentinel.py` changed — its public API was already correct, it was simply never called.
- **The window closes at the scheduled timeout beat whichever way it went.** `_resolve_link_timeout` drops the instrument from the index before evaluating, so a reply arriving a week later cannot retro-cancel a timeout that already fired and already moved state. Within a single virtual day, scheduled resolutions run before new beats (`_run_day`), so a reply on the same day-index as an expiry does not save it — day-granular timestamps cannot separate them, and "the window had already closed when the day began" is the reading that keeps `link_timed_out()`'s 48h meaning intact.

**Cost, stated plainly and measured, not predicted:** none to the recovery number — the 45-day run is **unchanged** at 21 KEPT / 27 HUMAN_HANDOFF / 3 DISPUTED and **₹23,31,496 (33.4%)**. What changed is the audit trail: 13 → **4** `mandate_refused` events, 10 → **4** entities barred from a future mandate offer, 13 → **4** soft-refusal entries. The full before/after table and the three-part explanation of why the money did not move (including one part of P10's diagnosis that measurement disproved — `trust.update_refusal` is pending-neutral, so no posterior moved) is in BUILD_LOG.md under the same date.

### 2026-08-26 — packet P9: the ten judgment calls inside the confidence gates + review queue
Master doc §2.3 states three gates in three lines and §3.6 states the queue in one; neither says how they compose with the bounds. Every call below is a decision the spec left open, made once, tested, and stated here rather than buried.

1. **The gate constants live in `ledger.py`, NOT in `state_machine.py`.** CLAUDE.md law 4 says "all bounds live as constants at the top of `state_machine.py`" — and these are not bounds. The bounds answer "is this action allowed?" and are absolute; the gates answer "do we trust what we READ enough to act unsupervised?", which is a property of the LLM boundary. Crucially the gates are strictly *weaker* than the bounds: they can only DEFER to a human, never permit. Nothing in `state_machine.py`'s bounds block was touched, and `check_bounds()` is unchanged.
2. **An extraction that carries no `confidence` at all is NOT gated.** The gate compares a number, and "we were told nothing" is not evidence of low confidence. Every producer inside the system supplies one (the runner writes it from the real `Extraction`); the ungated case is manual injection through `POST /events` in tests and demos. Pinned by `test_an_extraction_that_states_no_confidence_is_not_gated` so it stays a decision rather than becoming a surprise. A stale confidence is also cleared rather than carried forward.
3. **The money gate is scoped to the MANDATED branch only** — the one branch an *extraction* drives straight into money movement, which is exactly what §2.3 bullet 3 says ("any extraction that would trigger a MONEY action"). `mandate_execute` at AT_RISK is the one allowed retry of a mandate the debtor already approved (driven by a webhook, not by a reading of a sentence); the LINKED `link` is the post-refusal fallback; Tier-0 reserve capture involves no perception at all. **Both** candidates *inside* the MANDATED branch are gated, though — the offer and the link it falls back to when a bound refuses the offer — so a low-confidence read cannot reach the wire through the fallback door.
4. **The second sub-0.75 extraction holds a `message`/`stage:"clarify"` item rather than inventing a new action kind.** §2.3 says "still ambiguous after clarify → route to human-review queue" without saying what the human is approving. Holding the clarify message keeps "ONE clarifying question" true *of the agent* — it asks once and never again on its own — while giving the human the one lever that actually helps (ask again, with their eyes on the thread). No new `ActionKind` was added.
5. **A clarifying question is touch-counted like any outreach.** An agent that could ask "just to confirm?" for free would have found a way around bound #4. Costs us a real touch on ambiguous threads; that is the correct price.
6. **Reject on a held `mandate_offer` emits the payment-link fallback, and the fallback is NOT re-held.** The packet spec words reject as "falls back to the link/ladder path the same way a bounds-block does", and a bounds-block at MANDATED tries the link. Re-holding that link would be an infinite hold loop with a human at the bottom of it — and the click that just happened *is* the human review the money gate exists to obtain. The fallback still passes `check_bounds()`.
7. **A blocked approval returns HTTP 200 with `{"blocked": true, "block_reason": ...}`, not a 4xx.** The request was valid and the system did exactly what it should; a bound refused the action. Making it an error would teach the dashboard to treat a working stopping rule as a failure. The screen renders it in the error tone anyway, because the merchant's click did not achieve what they wanted.
8. **The formal-notice draft refuses BOTH approve and reject (403); `mark-handled` is its only button.** "Reject" on a notice the agent was never going to send is a meaningless state, and a 403 on both is one fewer way to misread the law. Enforced in the ledger (`sendable=False`), not only in the UI.
9. **`human_resolution` does not move trust.** A merchant closing a handoff as recovered/written-off is bookkeeping about OUR process, not new evidence about how this debtor behaves. Inventing a Beta update from an admin click would put a number in the trust curve that no debtor action earned. Promise records *are* closed (recovered → kept, written_off → broken) so law 5's "no silent deaths" holds on the promise ledger too.
10. **The pause kill-switch outranks the Tier-0 reserve debit.** A merchant who paused a thread has said "move no money on this", and a reserve capture is money moving, however few touches it costs. Pause is enforced inside the ledger's `_gate()` (the same chokepoint as the bounds), so it stops the thread everywhere, not just in the runner's outreach loop.

**Terminal-state immutability and its one exception.** `human_resolution` is the only event that can move a terminal state, and only from HUMAN_HANDOFF/DISPUTED to KEPT/CLEAN_LOSS. It is contained by construction, not convention: nothing in `engine/` emits it (asserted over a full 45-day run), `POST /events` refuses it with a 400 pointing at the real route, and `Ledger.resolve_handoff()` additionally refuses any entity that is not currently an open handoff or dispute. **It is deliberately EXCLUDED from `test_state_machine.py`'s 1000-walk fuzz pool** — that pool models event streams the *pipeline can actually produce*, and this one is not among them. Excluding it is the stricter choice: including it would have weakened the immutability tests into "immune to everything except the event we just added", whereas excluding it keeps them at "immune to every event the pipeline can produce" and pins the exception separately from both ends (`test_human_resolution_is_the_one_event_that_moves_a_terminal_state`, plus the two containment tests named above).

**Cost, stated plainly:** the 45-day heuristic run recovers **₹23,31,496 (33.4% of active-invoice value)** with nobody clicking, down from **₹25,59,383 (36.6%)** before P9 — two mandate offers that used to go out unsupervised now sit in a queue. With a merchant actually working the queue it goes the other way: see BUILD_LOG 2026-08-26 (P9) for the measured ₹31,74,725 / 26 KEPT with three approved clicks.

### 2026-08-26 — Provider layer built (packet P1): the judgment calls inside the heuristic rules
**What:** `engine/perception/providers/` now holds the `PerceptionProvider` ABC + registry (`get_provider()`: argument → `PK_PERCEPTION_PROVIDER` → `heuristic`), the `heuristic` / `anthropic` / `oracle` providers, and a file cache at `.cache/perception/`. `extractor.py` / `triage.py` / `cart_cause.py` keep their signatures and route through the registry. Four calls inside the rules provider needed a decision the spec didn't make; all four are documented in `HeuristicParams` and tunable there:
1. **`dispute` vs `delivery_dispute` is decided by SCOPE, not by topic.** Contesting the invoice/the obligation ("we're not paying this") is `dispute` even when the stated reason is a delivery complaint; a condition complaint with no refusal is `delivery_dispute`. Forced by a real contradiction between `prompts/triage.md` few-shot #3 and the T-12 ground-truth label — see BUILD_LOG entry the same date. **The lead must reconcile the prompt or the label; this only settles the rules side.**
2. **`non_responsive` requires outreach that went unanswered**, not merely "zero inbound messages". An invoice nobody has contacted yet is not a silent debtor. A bare "ok" is counted as silence-equivalent, not as a response (same `bare_ack_tokens` list the L5 extraction rule uses).
3. **The debtor's own words outrank the record flags.** `payment_failed_attempt` / `delivery_confirmed` only decide the cause when the debtor has never spoken — which is what `prompts/triage.md` already implies ("strong evidence... *even if the debtor hasn't said anything*"). `data/generate.py` flips those flags ~10% of the time on purpose, so a stale flag beating a live thread would be the wrong precedence.
4. **Hedged timing is not explicit timing.** "early next week" resolves to a date; "*maybe* early next week" resolves to None and drops a level. Direct application of the ladder's own rule (never invent a date), rather than a new one.
Also decided: the heuristic **never derives an amount the debtor didn't state** — "half now" produces a `condition` string, not a silently halved invoice total, even though the total is sitting in the thread (design law 2, applied one level earlier than it strictly had to be).
**Why it's safe:** no design law moves. Providers still only emit `Extraction` / `InvoiceCause` / `CartCause`; the ledger and state machine remain the only sources of amounts, dates and transitions.
**Cost / caveat we must state out loud:** the heuristic rules were authored **with visibility of the full hand-labelled set** — there is no held-out split, so its accuracy is an **in-sample upper bound**, not a generalisation estimate. `metrics/*_heuristic.json` carries `"in_sample": true` plus the caveat text, and both evals print it. Measured 2026-08-26: extraction **97.7%** (n=44, gate 85% → PASS), triage **71.7%** (n=60, gate 90% → FAIL; 91.7% on the 24 threaded invoices, 58.3% vs a 61.1% ceiling on the 36 with no thread — see BUILD_LOG). Cart cause 12/12 (not gated, no eval script exists).
**Eval integrity:** both evals REFUSE `--provider oracle` (exit 2, `CircularEvalRefused`) — replaying `data/ground_truth.json` and scoring it against `data/ground_truth.json` would report 100% by construction. Metrics filenames are now per provider (`metrics/extraction_accuracy_{provider}.json`) so a baseline and an LLM can sit side by side instead of overwriting each other.

### 2026-08-26 — LLM wiring: model id, temperature removed, structured outputs
**What:** CLAUDE.md/BUILD.md specify model `claude-sonnet-4-6` and "temp 0, JSON schema enforced" for every perception call. Both are stale against the `anthropic` Python SDK actually installed (1.0.0): `claude-sonnet-4-6` is a retired id (current Sonnet is `claude-sonnet-5`), and `temperature`/`top_p`/`top_k` have been removed from the Messages API entirely on Sonnet 5 / Opus 5 / Fable 5 — passing any of them is a 400. In their place, the SDK ships **structured outputs**: `client.messages.parse(..., output_format=<a pydantic model>)` returns `response.parsed_output` already validated against that model's schema server-side.
**Why this isn't a downgrade:** structured outputs is a *stronger* determinism/reliability guarantee than temp-0 sampling ever was — it's schema-validated by the API itself, not just low-entropy token sampling that could still drift off-schema. It's also a closer match to what BUILD.md was actually asking for ("JSON schema enforced") than manually parsing prose+JSON out of a temp-0 completion would have been.
**Fix:** `engine/perception/client.py` uses `MODEL = "claude-sonnet-5"` and `call_structured()` wraps `messages.parse()` with `output_format`. CLAUDE.md §6 updated to match. Confirmed via the `claude-api` skill and by introspecting the installed SDK's `message_create_params`/`json_output_format_param` source directly (no `temperature` field exists anywhere in the installed SDK's types).
**Cost:** none — this only affects the mechanism, not any prompt content or accuracy target. Still fully blocked on `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` for an actual live call (Phase B).

### 2026-08-26 — data/generate.py: the L1–L5 extraction ladder definitions
**What:** BUILD.md/master doc reference "L1–L5" levels throughout (e.g. the Acme Traders example extracts `{level: L1, amount: 40000, date: Fri, cond: null}`) but never define what distinguishes L1 from L2 from L3 etc. — that's deferred to Day-4 prompt-writing. Since the Day 1–2 dataset needs concrete per-message labels now, I fixed a 5-level ladder in `data/generate.py`:
- L1 — firm + unconditional: explicit amount AND explicit date
- L2 — firm but partially specific: only one of {amount, date} explicit
- L3 — conditional OR structured/partial: can't be captured by one amount+date pair — either contingent on a stated external condition ("once my client pays me"), or a split/partial-payment offer ("half now, half in 2 weeks"); the `condition` field carries the qualifying detail either way
- L4 — vague/soft acknowledgment: generic intent, no concrete amount/date
- L5 — no commitment: silence-equivalent, deflection, dispute, or refusal
**Why L3 covers two cases:** initially treated "conditional" and "partial-payment" as separate concerns while labeling the dataset, then caught during a self-review pass (BUILD.md's "judge mode" re-read) that a strict contingency-only L3 didn't fit the partial-payment thread (T-16) — broadened the definition rather than inventing a 6th level, since both cases share the same practical consequence: the state machine can't execute a single clean mandate from the message alone.
**Cost:** none to scope — same one-line-change-plus-relabel note as the InvoiceCauseType decision below applies here too if Day-4 prompt-writing (Phase B) wants a different split. BUILD.md's own fallback path (documented in BUILD.md Day 4) is "merge L2/L4" if accuracy is low, which is coherent under this ladder (L2 and L4 are the two levels most likely to be confused: both lack full amount+date specificity, differing mainly in how resolvable the vague part is).

### 2026-08-26 — data/generate.py: judge-mode self-review caught a real mislabel

### 2026-08-26 — packet P10: how "show the guardrails" was made impossible to lie with
**What:** `engine/judgment/state_machine.py` gained `check_bounds_detailed()` (a list of `BoundsCheck(name, passed, detail)`) and `engine/judgment/ledger.py` gained `gate_log: list[GateRecord]` — the full per-bound checklist recorded at the instant `_gate()` reached its verdict. `check_bounds()` itself is byte-for-byte unchanged: same signature, same short-circuit, same single reason, same tests.
**Why a second function instead of rewriting the gate to return a list:** a gate should stop at the first refusal — that is what makes it cheap and what makes its reason unambiguous. What a *human* needs is the opposite: every check, including the ones a short-circuit never reached, and the numbers each compared. Those are two different jobs, so they are two functions, bound together by a proven invariant rather than by a shared implementation: `check_bounds(x).allowed == all(c.passed for c in check_bounds_detailed(x))`, asserted over 5000 random (entity, action_kind, params, touch-window) combinations, **plus** that the first failing check is the bound `check_bounds()` names in its reason (`tests/test_state_machine.py::test_check_bounds_detailed_can_never_disagree_with_check_bounds`). Without that test the screen could show a checklist the decision never reached, which is CLAUDE.md law 8 broken in the most convincing possible way — with a tick next to it.
**Why the checklist is RECORDED rather than recomputed:** explaining a day-3 decision by re-running the bounds against today's entity state would put numbers on screen that the system never decided from — the touch window especially, which moves every day. `_gate()` therefore writes a `GateRecord` (params, verdict, checklist, and the audit entry id it produced) next to the verdict, and the API attaches it to the exact audit entry it belongs to. Nothing reads a `GateRecord` to decide anything; it is append-only bookkeeping.
**The merchant kill-switch is in the recorded checklist but NOT in `check_bounds_detailed()`.** It is not a hard bound (it is a human's pause, enforced in the ledger ahead of the bounds), so it stays out of `state_machine.py` — but a recorded checklist that omitted it would show "all bounds passed" beside a refusal. `_gate()` prepends it, which keeps `allowed == all(checks passed)` true for a `GateRecord` exactly as the invariant keeps it true for the bounds alone.
**Cost:** one extra `check_bounds_detailed()` call per gate (~259 gates over a 45-day run) and a per-day snapshot of entity + trust state in `WorldRunner.day_snapshots`. No behaviour change anywhere; 394 pre-existing tests unchanged and green.

### 2026-08-26 — packet P10: the Day Story's real-vs-simulated labelling, and the two places it refuses to guess
**What:** `GET /day/{n}/story`, `/debtors`, `/entities/{id}/conversation`, `/entities/{id}/guardrail-checks`, `/entities/{id}/mandate-timeline`, plus an additive `stories` field on `POST /advance`; all read-only, all served by one builder (`engine/integration/day_story.py`) so the advance payload and the day route cannot drift (`tests/test_day_story.py::test_advance_and_day_story_agree`).
**Labelling decision:** a single `real: bool` was not enough vocabulary to be honest with. "Mandate offered" is a real, audited decision but involves no Razorpay call; a registration link may be a genuinely real TEST-mode object; an execution outcome never is. So every mandate step carries a `nature` — `engine_decision` / `razorpay_real` / `razorpay_simulated` / `simulated_persona` / `simulated_outcome` — and the UI badge is driven by that, never by `real` alone. `real` is kept and means exactly one thing: a live Razorpay TEST-mode API call happened. TRACK_BAR §0's account gate is surfaced verbatim next to the Registered step, never in a footnote.
**Two refusals to invent, both visible in the API:**
  1. **Cart customers have no name.** `data/carts.json` stores a `customer_id` and nothing else, so `name` is `null`, `label` is the id itself, and `name_note` says why in a sentence. A plausible business name here would be a fabrication printed beside a real amount.
  2. **Trust "as of that day" had to be stored, not derived.** The ledger keeps one live posterior per debtor; re-deriving a past one from the trail would be a computation the system never actually performed. `WorldRunner._snapshot_day()` photographs entity + trust state at the end of each simulated day instead, and the story reads that. Days with no snapshot report `null` plus a note rather than borrowing today's number.
**Cost:** `POST /advance {"days": 45}` now returns ~785 KB (of which ~784 KB is `stories`) instead of ~700 bytes. Acceptable for a local judge dashboard and the price of the advance-and-day-story-agree guarantee; if it ever matters, the cap belongs on the number of days `stories` covers, not on the beats inside one.
